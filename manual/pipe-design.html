Pipe design

From a recorded line to a buried pipe design.

Record the surface along your route, tell Exodus the depth and slope limits your pipe must respect, and it works out the buried profile for you. Then add stations for the special spots.

Constraints you control

Burial depth range, optimal depth, slope limits, transition length and outlet location.

A chart you can read

Surveyed ground, the designed pipe and the allowed burial band. Trouble spots are marked in yellow with a suggested fix.

Stations for real jobs

Add a required depth to clear a crossing, a reducer where the pipe size changes, or cut the line short.

Your pipe, not ours

The inventory starts empty and you enter the pipes you actually lay.

Designing a pipe

A pipe design turns a recorded 3D line (the surface along the route) into a buried pipe: it works out how deep the pipe must be, and at what slope, along its whole length. The result is a design you can activate and grade to.

Open the Pipe Design screen

  1. Record a 3D line along the route (see Points of interest on a line for lines).
  2. Open the left panel and expand Lines 3D.
  3. Tap the profile icon (Design from <line name>) at the right of the line.

The Pipe Design screen opens on a copy of the line. Nothing is saved until you press Save.

The settings

The boxes along the top are the constraints the pipe must fit inside. The solver finds the pipe that stays inside them and stays as close to the optimal depth as it can. All depths are measured down from the ground surface.

SettingWhat it does
Min burial depthThe shallowest the pipe may be, for cover and frost. Default 0.5 m.
Max burial depthThe deepest the pipe may be, for what the machine can dig. Default 2.1 m.
Optimal depthThe depth the solver aims for wherever it can. Default 1.2 m.
Outlet depthHow deep the pipe is at the outlet end, where the water leaves. Default 1.2 m. Must be between min and max depth. See Measure outlet to take it from the plow bit.
Min transition lengthThe shortest distance over which the slope may change from the minimum to the maximum. A bigger number gives gentler bends in the pipe's profile. Default 20 m, allowed from 0.3048 m (1 ft) to 100 m.
Min slopeThe flattest the pipe may run, so water keeps moving. Default 0.5 %, cannot be below 0.
Max slopeThe steepest the pipe may run. Default 5 %, must be above the minimum.
Outlet locationWhich end of the line is the outlet: Line start or Line end. The pipe falls toward it.

If the settings contradict each other (for example, the minimum depth deeper than the optimal), the screen says so in plain words and the pipe is not built until it is fixed.

The profile chart

The chart is a side view along the line.

LineMeaning
Surveyed (dark green)The ground surface along your recorded line.
Pipe (bright green)The designed pipe, its flow line. A small square marks the outlet end.
Burial band (shaded)The zone between the minimum and maximum depths below the ground. The pipe must stay inside it.
Violation (yellow)A stretch where the pipe could not stay inside the band.

Tap a yellow stretch for details: how many stretches are outside, the worst one, and the fix suggested (for example Raise max burial depth by 0.3 m). Where the ground is too steep or too lumpy for the limits you set, loosen the limits, move the outlet, or add a required depth point.

Tap anywhere on the chart for the readout in Design stations.

The pipe row, and why Save is greyed out

A banner reads Pipe Required until you pick the pipe, because Save needs to know what pipe is being laid. Under the chart:

  • + Add pipe appears while the inventory is empty and opens the pipe inventory (see Pipe inventory).
  • Once pipes exist, the row reads Pick pipe. Tap it and choose the pipe for the first stretch. The gear opens the inventory.

Only pipes of the design's role (main, lateral or sub-lateral) are listed. Choosing the first pipe sets the design's role, and its saved settings (below) fill in the boxes.

Pipe roles and saved settings

The top-right of the screen shows the design's role (Main, Lateral or Sub-lateral) and a settings button.

  • Save <role> pipe config stores the current boxes as your starting point for every new design of that role.
  • Reset to my saved <role> config puts your saved boxes back.
  • If the boxes differ from the saved ones, the screen says so.

Measure outlet

Measure outlet sets the end of the design where the plow bit actually is. Use it when you have dug to the real outlet and want the design to start from there.

The button is the plow icon beside the Outlet depth box.

  1. Put the plow bit in the ground at the outlet.
  2. Tap the button.
  3. The outlet end of the line is moved to the bit. If the bit is past the end of the line, the line is extended out to it. If it is along the line, the part beyond the bit is cut off.
  4. The outlet depth is taken from the bit, and the design is built again.
Caution

Measuring the outlet throws away any stations you placed, because they were measured along the old line. Place stations after you measure.

The bit must be within 20 m of the end of the line. Otherwise the screen says Can't build - Measured outlet is too far from end of line. In the screenshot the bit is far from the line because the plow was running in Simulation. The button is not available when the design is a tie-in, because there the outlet is fixed by the pipe being joined.

Once the outlet has been measured, a button appears to Go back to the surveyed line and optimal outlet depth.

Design stations

A station is a marked spot along the pipe, measured as a distance from the start of the line. Stations tell the solver about special conditions, and tell the operator about changes that are coming up. They are drawn on the chart as vertical lines with a small marker above, and listed along the pipe row.

You make and edit stations from the profile chart on the Pipe Design screen.

Add a station

  1. Tap the chart where you want the station. A readout opens.
  2. Tap one of the three buttons at the bottom of the readout.

The readout shows:

RowMeaning
From start / From endDistance from each end of the line.
DepthThe pipe's depth below the ground at that spot.
SlopeThe slope of the pipe there.
Line elevThe elevation of the pipe's flow line.
Surface elevThe ground elevation.
Depth hereOnly when you tapped below the ground: the depth where you tapped. That is the depth a required depth station will be set to.

A station cannot be placed closer than 5 cm to another one, or to either end of the line.

The three buttons, left to right:

Cut (End of Run station)

The scissors cut the line at that spot. The part of the line farther from the outlet is folded away and is not part of the pipe.

  • The removed part is drawn hatched at the end of the chart, and the profile re-scales to the part that is left.
  • The design is solved again for the shortened line.
  • There can be only one cut. A new cut replaces the old one.
  • Hold the marker, then press the bin button, to delete the cut and restore the full line.

Pipe change (reducer)

The reducer button starts a new stretch of pipe at that spot, laid with the pipe that was there before. Use it when the pipe size changes.

  • The row under the chart now has one pipe selector for every stretch.
  • Tap a selector to pick a different pipe for that stretch. Only pipes of the design's role are offered.
  • A pipe change does not change the buried profile. It only changes which pipe is laid where, so the design is not solved again.
  • On the run screen the operator is warned as the machine nears it. If the size changes, it is called a reducer and shows the sizes either side (for example 152 mm → 102 mm).
  • Hold the marker, then press the bin button, to delete the station. The stretch it started goes back to the pipe before it.

Required depth

The required depth button (a pipe with a pin) forces the pipe through a particular depth at a particular spot, for example to clear a crossing or to go under a utility. It is only available where you tapped below the ground.

  1. Tap the chart below the ground surface at the place.
  2. Tap the required-depth button.
  3. A Depth box opens, pre-filled with the depth where you tapped. Type the depth you want, then tap the check mark.

The station is added and the design is solved again so the pipe passes through that point.

To change it later, hold the marker. Its card shows Required depth, its distance from the start and an editable Depth. Closing the card applies the new depth and solves again. The bin button deletes it.

If the pipe cannot reach a point within the slope limits and the outlet depth, the screen warns Required depth not reachable, and the pipe goes as near as it can. Move the point, or change the outlet depth or the slope limits.

Move a station

Touch and hold a station's marker and drag it along the chart. It stops short of its neighbours and of the ends. When you let go, a station that affects the buried pipe (a cut or a required depth) solves the design again. A pipe change does not.

Summary of the stations

StationButtonWhat it does
Required depthPipe and pinHolds the pipe at a depth at that spot. Solves again.
Pipe change (reducer when the size changes)ReducerStarts a new stretch with a different pipe. Does not change the buried profile.
End of run (cut)ScissorsRemoves the line beyond that spot. Only one. Solves again.

Save the design

Press Save. The design is saved to the project and is then available under Grade to in Grading mode. Save is only active once a pipe has been picked and the design is up to date with the settings. If you change a setting after the last build, press the Return key in the box (or change the value) so the design is solved again.

Pipe inventory

The pipe inventory is your list of the pipes you lay. Nothing is built in: it starts empty and you enter each pipe, so the design uses your real pipe.

Open it from the Pipe Design screen with + Add pipe, or with the gear beside the pipe row.

Add a pipe

  1. Choose what it is used as: Main, Lateral or Sub-lateral. This sets which designs can use it and is the starting point for a new design.
  2. Enter a Description that tells it apart in the list, for example HDPE corrugated 6in.
  3. Enter the Inside diameter in millimetres. This is the inside of the pipe, not the outside. It decides how much water the pipe carries.
  4. Leave Manning's roughness (n) at 0.011 (smooth corrugated plastic) unless the maker gives another value.
  5. Tap Add to inventory.

The pipe appears in the list on the left, grouped by role, with its diameter, role and roughness.

Edit a pipe

Tap the gear beside the pipe. The form loads with its values; change them and tap Save changes, or Cancel changes.

Delete a pipe

Tap the bin beside the pipe and confirm Delete.

Designs that already use the pipe keep their own copy, so deleting a pipe does not change a design that was built with it.
