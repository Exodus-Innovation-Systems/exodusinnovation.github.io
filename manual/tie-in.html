Tie-in: connecting a new pipe to an existing pipe

A tie-in makes a new pipe run into a pipe that has already been designed. The new pipe's outlet is fixed to the existing pipe, and the design offers an offset so the two meet properly.

There are two ways to start one:

  • Select it on the screen (this section).
  • Let Exodus suggest it while you record a line (see Pipe connection suggestion).

Select a tie-in by tapping the screen

  1. Tap the crosshair button in the right-hand column. A bar opens at the top reading Select tie-in point. A red reticle and a line to the machine show where it will connect.
  1. Tap the map where the new pipe should join. The reticle moves to the spot you tapped and a black guide line is drawn from the machine to it.
  1. If the route has to bend on the way, tap Add vertex to fix a corner at the reticle, then tap the next spot. Remove vertex takes back the last one. Each vertex is marked with a black reticle.
  1. Put the reticle on the existing pipe. When it is close enough, the reticle turns green, and the bar changes to Connect to this pipe from current location? It shows:
    ItemMeaning
    HeadingThe compass direction from where you are to the connection.
    DistanceHow far it is.
    Segment / TotalThe slope of the last leg and of the whole route.
    Pipe nameThe existing pipe it will join.

    A profile card also appears, showing the ground between the machine and the pipe and the existing pipe's depth where it joins.

  2. Tap Yes to connect, or Close to cancel.

Tapping Yes opens the Pipe Design screen (see The Pipe Design screen for a tie-in).

Next station readout

In the screenshot above, the pill at the top reads 152 MM → 102 MM - 33.28 m. That is the countdown to the next station on the active design, described in Countdown to a station while grading.

Pipe connection suggestion

While you record a line, Exodus watches where you are heading. When the line is heading toward an existing pipe, it asks:

Are you trying to connect to <pipe name>, <distance> away?

with Yes and Close buttons. It appears when the pipe is within about 30 m ahead and the approach is steep enough: a pipe straight ahead is offered from further out than one you are only skimming. A pipe that you are running along (a very shallow angle) is not offered.

In the screenshot the plow is recording a line (Recording Line in the Survey Options panel, and the line profile at the bottom) and is heading at the existing pipe Line3D1-pipe. Two things appear:

  • The banner at the top: Are you trying to connect to Line3D1-pipe, 27.83 m away? The distance is from the machine to the point where the line would meet the pipe, and it updates as you drive.
  • A green reticle on the ground, on the existing pipe, shows exactly where the new line would connect.
  • Yes ties the line into that pipe. Keep driving to the pipe and finish the line: the recording continues up to the pipe and the Pipe Design screen opens for the tie-in.
  • Close dismisses the suggestion and you carry on as a normal line.

The Pipe Design screen for a tie-in

When the design is a tie-in, the Pipe Design screen has some extras.

In the screenshot the new pipe is a 6 in (152 mm) pipe tying into an existing 152 mm pipe. Because the two are the same size, the default offset is 0.

ItemWhat it is
TitleTie-in to <existing pipe name>.
Tying intoUnder the offset box: the name of the existing pipe, its size and its invert (the elevation of the bottom inside of the pipe) where the new pipe joins, for example 152 mm pipe, invert 757.058 m.
Outlet depthShown under Tying into (for example Outlet depth 1.125 m). The Outlet depth box is replaced by the offset: you do not type the depth, it follows from the offset.
Offset above pipe invertTakes the place of the Outlet depth box, top right. How high above the existing pipe's invert the new pipe comes in. It starts at half of the difference between the existing pipe's diameter and the new pipe's, so a smaller pipe sits centred in the larger one. Change it to raise or lower where the new pipe enters.
Existing pipeThe existing pipe is marked at the outlet end of the chart with a label (for example EXISTING PIPE 152 mm) and listed in the legend as Existing pipe.
Measure outletNot offered: the outlet is the existing pipe.

Offsetting the adjoining pipe

The offset decides where the new pipe meets the existing one:

  • 0 puts the bottoms of the two pipes at the same level.
  • Half the difference in diameters centres the new pipe in the existing one (the default).
  • A number that makes the new pipe's top go above the existing pipe's top shows a violation: Pipe will be <x> above the pipe it's tying into.
  • A negative number comes in below the existing pipe's invert: This pipe will come in below the invert of the pipe it was meant to tie into.

The violation is a warning only. It does not stop you building or saving the design.

You still pick the new pipe from the inventory, add stations, and press Save as for any design. The pipe that meets the existing one is the pipe at the outlet end of the line, so if you add a reducer near the connection, the size that matters is the one at the end.
